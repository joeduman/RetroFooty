# Gameplay references reviewed

All six supplied recordings were accessed with yt-dlp after the web fetch was blocked. FFmpeg extracted reference frames, which were visually inspected. The observations below come from those frames, not video titles or imagined play. Playback is treated as normal speed; the three recent highlight videos contain visible edits and transitions. No movement-speed or stamina-drain estimate was derived across a cut.

Review scope: 24-second windows at **00:06–00:30** for five sources and **18:08–18:32** for the requested Olympics 4v4 source. Overview sheets sample every two seconds. Three important sequences were inspected again at 3–4 frames/second. This is a sampled review, not a frame-by-frame review of every minute of all six videos. Video times below are approximate (within about one second); the on-screen match clock is a different clock.

The public recordings do not identify this repository's current Expert bots. No current-build Expert gameplay or 2v2 sequence could be verified from the sampled windows. Exact controls, boost stocks, intended tactics, and game-world coordinates cannot be read reliably from these recordings. Tactical purpose is an interpretation; visible actions and scores are observations.

The strongest verified individual sequence in this sample is the first 1v1 diagonal finish. The clearest verified team sequence is Iby's touchline pass to Arecki followed by the left-goal finish. The clearest corner combination is WhereAreYou's upper-side pressure followed by Arecki's separated central finish. The 3v3 tournament sample is useful defensive evidence because a challenger and covering player stay at different depths. These are best **within the reviewed excerpts**, not claims about every play in all footage.

A wall rebound is visible in several highlight approaches, but this review does not reliably identify a clean scoring bank shot or calibrated bank angle. That behavior must therefore be validated against the current engine's wall restitution and drag, not inferred from the old footage. No confident example of a complete give-and-go, a measured boost trap, deliberate feigned vulnerability, or a full sweeper-keeper rotation was established in the sampled material. Those requested behaviors remain engine tests and future video-review targets.

The practical design lessons are consistent across the inspected modes: plan the next touch before accelerating, calculate whether the defender can reach its exit path, keep an off-ball player outside local pressure, and retain a recovery route after a challenge. The footage supports these principles; only in-engine measurements can establish that a particular runtime implementation performs them better.
